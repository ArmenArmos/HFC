## 1. What the Armenian document is

It is the **"Գործելակարգ զարկերակային գերճնշմամբ (ԶԳՃ) պացիենտի վարման"** – *Procedure (standard of care) for the management of a patient with arterial hypertension* – issued as the annex to **Order No 74-N of the RA Minister of Health, 21 December 2023** (p. 1). It covers adults with ICD-10 **I10 (essential hypertension) and I15.0–I15.9 (secondary hypertension)** (p. 1), in outpatient-polyclinic and day-hospital settings, as planned (non-emergency) care, for family physicians, therapists and cardiologists (p. 4).

Its only cited source is the **2023 ESH Guidelines** (p. 17). It is a *resource and service standard* in the Armenian MoH "գործելակարգ" format: ICD codes, BP classification, complication list, complaints and history to take, hospitalisation indications, and five forms listing which consultations, tests, drugs and procedures are given and **how often per 100 cases** (frequency 0–1). It is **not a decision algorithm**: it contains no risk score, no treatment thresholds or targets, no home-BP cut-offs, no visit intervals and no lifestyle advice.

## 2. Bottom line

The two documents overlap on only part of their ground, and where they overlap they mostly agree. The Armenian protocol is shallower than the Noreon tree in every "what to do" area, but broader in "what to check". Ranked by how much it would change what Noreon tells members or doctors:

1. **Different BP category names and diastolic cut-offs below 140/90.** The protocol uses the ESH 2023 scheme: optimal <120 and <80, normal 120–129 and 80–84, high-normal 130–139 and/or 85–89 (protocol pp. 2–3; same scheme described in [Yang24] and the ACC analysis). The tree uses the ESC 2024 scheme: non-elevated <120/70, elevated 120/70 to <140/90, hypertension ≥140/90 ([ESC24] Table 5, §5.1; tree P167–174, P680–682). A member with 125/75 is "elevated" in Noreon and "normal" under the Armenian protocol; 118/78 is "normal" in Noreon but "optimal" in Armenia. Grades 1–3 (140/90, 160/100, 180/110) are identical in both guidelines and both documents.
2. **The protocol expects a wider baseline work-up than the tree orders, and so does ESC 2024.** The protocol lists echocardiography for every patient (1/1), fundoscopy 0.7/1, 24-h ABPM 0.5/1, plus haemoglobin, sodium, uric acid, fasting glucose and urinalysis (pp. 9–11). The tree orders ECG, lipids, HbA1c, eGFR, urine ACR, K+/creatinine and Lp(a), and an echo only after ECG LVH (P394–410, P579–581). Verified against ESC 2024: its routine set (Table 8) is fasting glucose (HbA1c if raised), lipids, **sodium, potassium, haemoglobin/haematocrit, calcium, TSH**, creatinine/eGFR, **urinalysis and ACR**, 12-lead ECG; echocardiography is Class I B only "in patients with hypertension and ECG abnormalities, or signs or symptoms of cardiac disease"; fundoscopy is Class I C only if BP >180/110 or in diabetes (Table 9; Recommendation Tables 8–9). So: the tree is narrower than *both* guidelines on routine labs (no Hb, Na, Ca, TSH, fasting glucose, urinalysis); the protocol is stricter than ESC 2024 on echo and fundoscopy; uric acid is in the protocol but in neither ESC 2024 Table 8 nor (as far as secondary sources show) the tree. Touches C5 and C12.
3. **No cardiovascular risk score at all in the protocol.** SCORE2, the risk categories and LDL-C targets that drive the tree's Phase 2 (P414–506) have no counterpart. ESH 2023 itself does endorse SCORE2 (40–69) and SCORE2-OP (≥70) for risk stratification ([Yang24]; ACC analysis), so this is an omission of the national procedure, not of its source. The protocol instead uses **hypertension stages 1–3** (p. 3), an ESH 2023 construct that Noreon never produces (touches C9).
4. **No treatment thresholds or BP targets in the protocol.** The tree's rules are ESC 2024's and are verified: drug treatment for confirmed hypertension ≥140/90 (Class I); for elevated BP with "sufficiently high" risk (10-year CVD risk ≥10%, or established CVD, diabetes, CKD, FH or HMOD, or 5–<10% plus risk modifiers), after 3 months of lifestyle measures, drugs for confirmed BP ≥130/80 (Class I A); target systolic 120–129 mmHg if tolerated (Class I A) ([ESC24] Table 14, Recommendation Tables 17–18, §8.4–8.5). ESH 2023 is more conservative: drugs in the high-normal band only for patients with established CVD, especially coronary disease; targets <130/80 at 18–64, <140/80 at 65–79 (then <130/80 if tolerated), SBP 140–150 at ≥80 ([Yang24]; ACC analysis; primary not readable). The protocol is silent, so which of the two its users follow is a clinical decision (touches C13).
5. **The protocol is scoped to secondary hypertension and pregnancy; the tree has no branch for either.** It asks for young onset (<40 with grade 2–3), hypokalaemia, pheochromocytoma symptoms, thyroid/parathyroid disease, pregnancy/pre-eclampsia, hormonal contraception, and BP-raising drugs (pp. 5–6). The tree collects none of this. The tree's red-flag logic (≥180/110 plus symptoms → 103) matches the ESC 2024 definition of hypertensive emergency ("BP of ≥180/110 mmHg associated with acute HMOD, often in the presence of symptoms", §10.1.1) and the protocol's hospitalisation indications; the protocol adds pregnancy and drug-induced crises.
6. **Noreon is richer than the protocol on everything the protocol does not cover**: lifestyle, chest pain, palpitations, sleep apnoea, stress, smoking, alcohol, diabetes and lipids. Nothing in the protocol contradicts this content.

Overall judgement: **few direct conflicts, many gaps in both directions.** The Armenian protocol can be layered under the tree as a "national baseline" (naming, stages, required tests, drug list, consultation set) without replacing the tree's decision logic, provided Armen settles the items in section 4.

## 4. Things Armen must decide

1. **Which BP category scheme Armenian members see below 140/90**: ESC 2024 (non-elevated / elevated, tree as is) or ESH 2023 / MoH (optimal / normal / high-normal with 80 and 85 diastolic cut-offs). Touches C1 and C13.
2. **Whether the 130/80 drug-start rule for high-risk members stays** for Armenian members. It is ESC 2024 Class I A (after 3 months of lifestyle measures, confirmed BP); ESH 2023 restricts drugs in the high-normal band to patients with established CVD; the national protocol is silent. Options: ESC for all; ESC only in the international profile; ESH rule for Armenian members. Touches C13.
3. **Which BP target the doctor view shows**: ESC 2024 systolic 120–129 (tree as is) or ESH 2023's age-dependent targets (<130/80 at 18–64; <140/80 then <130/80 if tolerated at 65–79; 140–150 systolic at ≥80). Touches C13.
4. **What "organ damage absent" requires**: the tree's three items (ECG LVH, ACR, eGFR; these match ESC 2024) or the protocol's wider set (echo for all, fundoscopy). Decides whether a Noreon risk category can be finalised without an echo. Touches C5 and C12.
5. **Which baseline tests are mandatory in the Armenian lab order**: protocol Form 3 (adds Hb, Na, uric acid, fasting glucose, urinalysis) and/or ESC 2024 Table 8 (adds Hb, Na, Ca, TSH, fasting glucose, urinalysis; no uric acid) on top of the tree's set. Note that the Noreon lab protocol v2 (section 6) already proposes a merged list; the decision is whether the tree and the lab protocol are reconciled. Touches C12.
6. **Confirmation method**: whether 7-day HBPM counts as confirmation for Armenian members (ESC 2024 says ABPM and/or HBPM, Class I B) or whether ABPM is ordered when available, as the protocol's frequencies imply. Touches C4.
7. **Whether Noreon records a national hypertension stage (1/2/3)** alongside the ESC risk category, and who assigns it. Touches C1 and C8.
8. **Whether to add secondary-hypertension, pregnancy and BP-raising-drug questions** (young onset, hypokalaemia symptoms, pregnancy or contraception, NSAIDs/decongestants/stimulants). New item; no C-number.
9. **Whether the Form 4 drug list becomes the reference formulary in the doctor summary** (shown as "national list", not as a recommendation). New item.
10. **Specialist routing for complicated hypertension**: whether a member with HMOD, CVD or CKD gets the protocol's four consultations as tasks, and in what time frame (ESC 2024 gives 1–3-monthly visits until control, yearly after). Touches C10.
11. **Which symptoms go on the ≥180/110 crisis checklist**: confirm it includes the ESC 2024 §10.1.1 list (headache, visual disturbance, chest pain, breathlessness, dizziness, neurological deficit, seizures/confusion). Touches C7 and RF-03.

## 5. How we could support both (international vs Armenian)

Non-technical; no code changes proposed.

- **One decision tree, two "profiles".** Keep v1.1 as the international (ESC 2024 / EAS 2025) profile. Define an Armenian profile as a short override list: category names and diastolic cut-offs below 140/90, drug-start rule in the 130–139 band, target wording, mandatory baseline test set, organ-damage evidence required, confirmation method, specialist set for complicated cases, national stage label. Everything not overridden (red flags, lifestyle, lipids, diabetes, sleep, chest pain) stays shared.
- **Where the profile is chosen.** Simplest is per clinic or country of care (Davidyants → Armenian profile), with a doctor override. A per-member setting is possible but creates mixed cohorts and harder QA.
- **What the member sees.** Only category wording, recheck interval and (if chosen) the treatment-threshold message change. Red-flag behaviour (103 screen) must be identical in both profiles; it is the same in ESC 2024, ESH 2023 and the protocol.
- **What the doctor sees.** The Armenian profile shows the national stage, the protocol's test list with done/missing, and Form 4 drug names as a reference. The ESC risk category and LDL target can still be shown as "international" information.
- **Governance.** Each profile needs a signed source: v1.1 for international (Tatevik, per C1); the MoH protocol plus Armen's answers to section 4 for Armenian. Record the profile used on every published plan.
- **Dark first.** Ship the Armenian profile behind a flag and verify on prod-as-staging before Davidyants members are enrolled (go-live gates register).

Net: the lab protocol v2 already does the "national baseline plus ESC supplement" reconciliation for *tests* that this report recommends for the *tree*, and it is more conservative than v1.1 on medication monitoring and on asymptomatic severe BP. Its English review also lists P0 corrections (electrolytes before medication, critical-result routing, no automatic diagnoses) that overlap with the 15 October audit findings.

## 7. Confidence and limits

- **Armenian protocol translation.** All 17 pages were read as page images; no OCR layer. Numbers (thresholds, frequencies, doses) are read directly and I am confident in them. Two phrases remain uncertain: the physical-examination line on p. 6 (read as "pulse on radial palpation; during an angina attack often pale skin and cold sweat; signs of HMOD where present") and one complaint on p. 4 read as "sensation of thirst". Neither affects a verdict.
- **ESC 2024: verified from the primary text.** Every ESC 2024 statement above was checked against the published article text (Table 5, Table 8, Table 9, Table 14, Recommendation Tables 5, 6, 8, 9, 17, 18, 19, §6.3.1, §7.4.3.1, §8.3.4, §10.1.1). The copy used is a repository reproduction of the Eur Heart J PDF; page and table numbers are the journal's. Class/level annotations are quoted from the recommendation tables.
- **ESH 2023: primary text not readable; verified from secondary sources.** The J Hypertens full text is subscription-only on every route tried (publisher page HTTP 402, Europe PMC "subscription required", university repository and ESH site copies blocked or absent). The statements attributed to ESH 2023 — classification (optimal/normal/high-normal, grades, ISH), stages 1–3, drug initiation in the high-normal band only with established CVD, age-dependent targets, SCORE2/SCORE2-OP endorsement, 3-monthly then yearly follow-up — are supported by the Armenian protocol's own tables (pp. 2–3, which reproduce the ESH classification), by the peer-reviewed open-access comparisons [Yang24] (PMC11399577) and [Grassi24] (PMC11263159), and by the ACC expert analysis of 5 Feb 2024. These are consistent with each other. Two ESH 2023 items could **not** be verified from any source: whether ESH 2023 itself recommends echocardiography and fundoscopy for all patients (the protocol's 1/1 and 0.7/1 may be the MoH's choice rather than ESH's), and whether the 5/3/1-year screening ladder on protocol pp. 11–12 is ESH wording. They are labelled as such in the table. [Grassi24] paraphrases older-patient thresholds/targets loosely ("160/90", "not below 130/80 or better 135/85"); where it and [Yang24] differ, [Yang24] (which quotes class and level) was used.
- **Earlier wording removed.** The first version of this report said the ESH-vs-ESC statements were "from memory". They are now sourced as above; no ESC 2024 statement needed correction. One statement was sharpened: the first version implied the tree's routine labs were only narrower than the Armenian protocol; they are also narrower than ESC 2024 Table 8 (no Hb, Na, Ca, TSH, fasting glucose, urinalysis). One was added: the tree's ≤1-week doctor deadline for grade 2 is tighter than ESC 2024's "within 1 month" confirmation window.
- **Frequencies.** The protocol's 1 / 0.5 / 0.2 values are averaged frequencies per 100 cases (pp. 8, 12, 16, 17). I read "1" as "expected in every case"; the document does not define them as individual obligations.
- **Decision tree side.** Compared against the v1.1 document, not the running app. Where the app deviates from v1.1 (15 October audit: grade 2 deadline, pack-year boundary, Phase 2 not executed), the gap to the Armenian protocol is at least as large as stated here.
- **Lab protocol v2.** Read from the document XML; tables were flattened, so section numbers are those printed in the text. It was not cross-checked against the lab catalogue JSON in the repo.
- **Other national protocols.** Armenia's MoH documents for diabetes, dyslipidaemia and ACS were not reviewed.
- **No clinical judgement.** "Stricter / looser" compares what each document asks for, not which is medically better.
